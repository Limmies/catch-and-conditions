A few taps on your Garmin. A record that grows with every trip. More to learn from when you get back on the water.

The idea behind Catch and Conditions is simple: make fishing data easy to collect, then turn it into something useful. Your watch records the catch, time and location. The journal brings those details together with conditions, so each trip adds to a picture of what works.

02 / From wrist to journal

Fish first. Keep a record as you go.

The current setup pairs a Garmin fēnix 5X with Garmin Connect on the phone and this private web journal.

  1. 1

    On the watch

    Choose a fishing style, start a trip and select a species to save a catch when GPS is usable. The record is kept locally on the watch.

  2. 2

    Across the connection

    With Garmin Connect, Bluetooth and internet available, the watch sends its saved records. Unsent records stay queued for another attempt.

  3. 3

    In your journal

    Sign in to see uploaded catches, completed trips and sessions. The website brings together the map, effort, spot comparisons and conditions.

The details you don’t have to write down

A catch’s time and GPS location let the journal look up weather for that moment and place. Add your own day ratings for what you actually experienced. Together, they give each catch more context than a species name alone.

03 / The plan

Build a better picture over time.

If you can only fish on holidays or the occasional weekend, every day on the water matters. Keeping an easy, consistent record means you can learn from past trips instead of starting from memory each time. The quiet sessions count too: they help show how much fishing went into each catch.

The long-term goal is to choose a date and get a useful suggestion: which spot to try, which species to target and why the conditions might suit it. Those predictions are still ahead of us. For now, we’re building the catch, effort and conditions history needed to find meaningful patterns over time.

Less time entering data. More to learn from the time we get to fish.

How to read this map

Gaussian catch density uses the same method as Map → Species. The radius is in metres and can be adjusted. Colours show relative recorded catch concentration, not fish abundance or fishing probability. Smoothing can extend onto land. No fishing-effort adjustment or habitat mask is applied.

Data quality & interpretation

Each chart reports its sample. Missing or unrecognised measurements are excluded, never replaced with zero. Calm currents and zero lunar phase are valid. Conditions are matched within one hour of the catch; only compatible units are compared. Repeated snapshots do not create extra catches.

Spot rates use recorded fishing sessions, including zero-catch sessions. Unclassified time is excluded from fishing hours. Small samples and incomplete GPS or recording can change rankings. No confidence intervals, causal claims or calibrated fishing predictions are implied. These charts include any test records still in your account; remove those before interpreting real fishing patterns.
